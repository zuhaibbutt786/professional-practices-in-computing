Introduction to Professional Practices in Computing

Why professionalism matters, what it means to be a computing professional, and how this course prepares you for ethical and responsible practice.

⏱ 75–90 min 📚 Core 🎯 Foundation ✍️ Reflection + Quiz
🎯

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Explain why professional practices are essential for computing graduates in CS, SE, DS, AI, and cybersecurity.
  • Distinguish between technical competence and professional competence.
  • Describe the dual role of a computing professional: creator of technology and steward of its societal impact.
  • Identify the main themes of the course and how they map to ACM/IEEE guidelines.
💡

Motivation

Technology is no longer neutral. Every design decision, data collection practice, model deployment, and security choice carries ethical, legal, and social consequences. Professional Practices in Computing exists because technical skill alone is insufficient.

Employers, regulators, and society increasingly expect computing graduates to reason about responsibility, privacy, fairness, safety, and long-term impact.

📖

Real Story

In 2018, the Cambridge Analytica scandal revealed how personal data from millions of Facebook users was harvested and used for political targeting without proper consent. Engineers and product managers who built the underlying APIs and data pipelines faced questions about professional responsibility that went far beyond “the code worked.”

This course prepares you so that when you face similar decisions, you have frameworks, codes, and judgment to act as a professional rather than only as a technician.

🧠

Key Concepts

Professionalism in computing means combining technical excellence with ethical judgment, legal awareness, communication skill, and accountability for the consequences of the systems you help create.

Professional identity is the internal sense that you are not merely a coder or analyst, but a member of a profession that holds itself to higher standards than the minimum required by law.

⚖️

Multiple Perspectives

  • Ethical: What ought we to do?
  • Legal: What must we do / avoid?
  • Business: What creates sustainable value while managing risk?
  • AI / Data Science: How do bias, opacity, and scale change responsibility?
  • Software Engineering: How do process and architecture embed values?
  • Cybersecurity: How does the duty to protect relate to other duties?
