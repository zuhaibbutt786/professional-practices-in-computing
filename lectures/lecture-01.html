Introduction to Professional Practices in Computing

Why professionalism matters in computing, building professional identity, and the roadmap for ethical and responsible technology work.

Learning Objectives

  • Explain why professional practices are essential in modern computing careers.
  • Distinguish between technical competence and professional responsibility.
  • Identify key stakeholders affected by computing decisions.
  • Recognize the scope of this course across ethics, law, society, and career development.

Introduction

Professional Practices in Computing prepares students to act as responsible creators and stewards of technology. Technical excellence is necessary but not sufficient; professionals must also reason about ethics, law, risk, stakeholders, and long-term societal impact.

Motivation — Why This Course Exists

Computing systems influence privacy, safety, fairness, employment, and democratic processes. Failures often arise from gaps in professional judgment, incomplete risk analysis, weak organizational culture, and insufficient attention to human and societal consequences.

Real Story — The Cost of Narrow Focus

Historical and contemporary incidents—from safety-critical software failures to large-scale privacy and fairness harms—show that concentrating only on feature delivery while neglecting verification, accountability, and stakeholder impact produces serious professional and societal costs.

Industrial Scenario

Imagine you are an early-career engineer asked to ship a feature under tight deadline pressure that affects user data handling. Professional practice requires you to surface risks, seek clarification on requirements and constraints, document concerns, and escalate when necessary rather than silently accepting incomplete safeguards.
