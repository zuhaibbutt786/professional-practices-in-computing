Lecture 01

Introduction to Professional Practices in Computing

Why professionalism matters, the unique responsibilities of computing graduates, and a map of the course ahead.

⏱ ~45–60 min📚 Module 1 of 30
◎

Learning Objectives

  • Explain why computing requires professional practices beyond technical skill.
  • Distinguish personal ethics, professional ethics, and organizational ethics.
  • Identify the main stakeholder groups affected by computing decisions.
  • Describe the structure and purpose of this course.
  • Articulate a preliminary professional identity as a computing graduate.
💡

Motivation

Software, data systems, and AI now mediate almost every aspect of modern life — healthcare, finance, transportation, education, justice, and public discourse. A single design choice, training dataset bias, or incomplete risk analysis can affect millions of people. Technical excellence alone is not enough; professionals must also exercise judgment, responsibility, and ethical reasoning.

Key insight
Computing is a socio-technical practice. Code does not exist in a vacuum; it shapes (and is shaped by) people, organizations, laws, and power structures.
📖

Real Story

In 2018, a major social platform faced global scrutiny after it was revealed that third-party data had been used for political profiling without adequate consent or transparency. Engineers and data scientists who built the underlying APIs and recommendation systems were not the decision-makers at the board level, yet their technical work enabled the outcome. The incident raised lasting questions about duty of care, secondary use of data, and the professional obligations of those who design powerful platforms.

Similar patterns appear across domains: safety-critical avionics, healthcare AI triage tools, autonomous vehicle perception stacks, and financial trading algorithms. In each case, professionals must ask not only “Can we build it?” but “Should we, under what constraints, and with what safeguards?”

🏭

Industrial Scenario

You join a product team shipping a new recommendation feature. Marketing wants maximum engagement. Legal wants compliance language. Security wants least privilege. Users want relevance without feeling manipulated. Your manager asks you to ship by the end of the sprint. How do you balance these pressures while protecting users and your own professional integrity?

This course gives you frameworks, codes, case studies, and decision processes so you can answer such questions with clarity rather than improvisation.

🧭

What “Professional Practices” Means

Professional practices in computing encompass:

  • Ethical reasoning — applying moral frameworks and professional codes to concrete dilemmas.
  • Legal literacy — understanding high-level obligations around privacy, IP, liability, and safety.
  • Responsibility & accountability — knowing who is answerable for outcomes and when to escalate or refuse.
  • Communication & teamwork — documenting, presenting, collaborating across roles and cultures.
  • Lifelong learning — staying current as technology, regulation, and societal expectations change.

The course is technology-independent. It teaches professional thinking rather than any specific language, framework, or cloud vendor.

🔍

Multiple Perspectives

⚖️ Ethical

What obligations do we owe users, non-users, future generations, and the public interest?

📜 Legal

What statutes, regulations, and case law constrain design and deployment choices?

💼 Business

How do short-term incentives interact with long-term trust, brand, and risk?

🤖 AI

How do autonomy, opacity, and scale change the nature of professional responsibility?

📊 Data Science

What duties arise from data collection, labeling, modeling, and secondary use?

🛡️ Security

How do confidentiality, integrity, and availability trade off against usability and innovation?
