Lecture 01 — Introduction to Professional Practices

Professionalism in computing is not optional. It is the foundation that protects users, organizations, and society from the consequences of poorly designed, unethical, or irresponsible systems.

Learning Objectives

  • Define professional practices in the context of computing disciplines.
  • Explain why ethical and professional competence is required by ACM and IEEE.
  • Identify the multi-perspective lens used throughout the course (ethical, legal, business, AI, data science, software engineering, cybersecurity).
  • Describe the structure and expected outcomes of the course.

Motivation

Every major computing failure of the last two decades — from privacy scandals to biased AI systems to safety-critical software defects — has roots in failures of professional judgment, not merely technical skill.

Real Story

When a social platform optimized engagement without sufficient regard for societal harm, the resulting information environment affected elections, public health, and mental health at scale. Engineers and product managers later reflected that the professional obligation to consider downstream effects had been secondary to growth metrics.

Industrial Scenario

You join a team building a recommendation system for a health-related app. Early data shows the model amplifies anxiety-inducing content for a vulnerable cohort. What professional steps do you take, and how do you escalate if business priorities conflict with user wellbeing?

Multi-Perspective Analysis

This course trains you to examine every major topic through ethical, legal, business, AI, data science, software engineering, and cybersecurity lenses simultaneously.

Knowledge Check

Professional practices in computing primarily concern:

  • Only writing correct code
  • Ethical reasoning, responsibility, communication, and societal impact alongside technical competence
  • Passing certification exams

Reflection

Identify one decision you have already made (or observed) in a computing project that had ethical or professional dimensions. What perspective was missing?

Summary

Professional Practices in Computing prepares you to act as a responsible computing professional. The course is technology-independent and focuses on judgment, codes, law, communication, and the societal impact of the systems you will design and operate.
