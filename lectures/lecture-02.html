Module 02

History of the Profession & Codes

How computing moved from craft and research culture to a profession with associations, accreditation, and codes of ethics—and what failures drove those changes.

Reading ~35 minTimeline + Quiz
🎯

Learning Objectives

  • Outline key stages in the professionalization of computing.
  • Explain why professional societies created codes of ethics.
  • Connect historical incidents to modern expectations of accountability.
  • Relate ACM/IEEE history to current student and practitioner obligations.
📖

Introduction

Professions form when society grants autonomy in exchange for expertise, standards, and public-regarding duties. Computing’s path included research labs, commercial software, professional societies (ACM, IEEE Computer Society), curriculum guidelines, and ethics codes updated as technology scaled into daily life.

⏱️

Brief Timeline

Mid-20th century: Computing as specialized scientific and military craft.

1960s–80s: Societies expand; software engineering debates; early codes and curricula.

1990s–2000s: Internet scale, privacy and IP conflicts, stronger professional guidelines.

2010s–2020s: AI, platforms, and data systems push updates to ethics guidance and regulation awareness.

🧭

Why Codes Appeared

Codes respond to repeated patterns: systems that harm the public, conflicts of interest, weak accountability, and the need for shared language when individuals must push back on organizations. History shows codes lag technology—but they still provide a common professional baseline.

Reflection

📋

Summary

  • Professionalization ties expertise to public-regarding standards.
  • Codes emerged from growth of impact and need for shared duties.
  • History is a warning system: scale without responsibility repeats harm.
