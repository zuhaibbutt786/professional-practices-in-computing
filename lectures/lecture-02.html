Lecture 02 — History of the Profession & Codes of Ethics

Understanding how computing became a profession and why codes of ethics emerged helps professionals act with historical awareness and institutional responsibility.

Learning Objectives

  • Trace the historical development of computing as a profession
  • Explain the role of early codes of ethics in professionalization
  • Compare key milestones in ACM and IEEE ethical guidance
  • Apply historical lessons to contemporary professional dilemmas

Why History Matters

Professional practices are not invented from scratch each decade. They accumulate through accidents, scandals, standards bodies, academic programs, and public expectations. Knowing this history prevents repeating known failures and strengthens judgment when new technologies (AI, autonomous systems, large-scale data) create novel risks.

Case Study Snapshot

Historical episodes such as early software reliability failures, the development of professional societies, and the formalization of codes after visible harms illustrate how the community learned to self-regulate. Students should examine primary statements from ACM and IEEE and discuss how wording evolved with societal expectations.

Knowledge Check

Reflection

  1. Which historical event most strongly shaped your view of professional responsibility in computing?
  2. How should codes of ethics adapt when the dominant risks shift from software reliability to AI societal impact?

Assignment

Write a 600–800 word analysis comparing one early code of ethics with a current ACM or IEEE statement. Identify continuity and change, and argue what the changes reveal about the profession’s self-understanding.

Cheat Sheet

  • Profession ≈ specialized knowledge + service orientation + self-regulation
  • Codes encode collective expectations and provide accountability anchors
  • History of computing ethics is driven by both technical failures and social harms

Glossary

Professionalization
Process by which an occupation develops formal knowledge, standards, and self-governance.
Code of ethics
Public statement of principles and expected conduct adopted by a professional body.
