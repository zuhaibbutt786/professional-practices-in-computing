Lecture 02

History of the Computing Profession & Codes of Ethics

How computing became a profession, why codes of ethics emerged, and what they aim to achieve.

⏱ ~50 min📚 Module 2 of 30
◎

Learning Objectives

  • Trace key milestones in the professionalization of computing.
  • Explain why formal codes of ethics were created for computing.
  • Compare early codes with modern ACM and IEEE codes at a high level.
  • Identify limitations of codes and the need for judgment beyond rules.
📜

Historical Background

1940s–50s

Early computing as scientific and military craft. Little formal professional identity.

1960s–70s

Software crisis, growing commercial systems, first discussions of professional standards and liability.

1972 / 1992

ACM Code of Ethics evolves; IEEE develops codes emphasizing public welfare and safety.

2018

ACM Code substantially revised for the age of AI, data, and global platforms.

Unlike medicine or law, computing professionalized after its tools already had massive social impact. Codes therefore arrived as a response to observed harms and public expectations, not only as internal guild rules.

🎯

Why Codes Exist

Codes of ethics serve several functions:

  • Guidance — help practitioners reason about novel situations.
  • Accountability — provide a shared reference for criticism and self-regulation.
  • Public trust — signal that the profession accepts duties beyond employer interests.
  • Education — orient students and new professionals to expected standards.
Limitation
Codes are not algorithms. They require interpretation, prioritization when principles conflict, and moral courage when organizational pressure is high.
🏭

Industrial Scenario

A mid-size company ships a mobile app that collects more location data than disclosed in the privacy notice. Marketing argues “everyone does it.” Legal is under-resourced. You are a junior engineer who notices the discrepancy. What resources (codes, internal policies, escalation paths) can you draw on, and what risks do you face?
