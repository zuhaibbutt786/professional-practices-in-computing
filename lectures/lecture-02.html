Lecture 02 — History of the Profession & Codes of Ethics

How computing became a profession, why codes of ethics emerged, and what they demand of practitioners today.

Learning Objectives

  • Trace key milestones in the professionalization of computing.
  • Explain why formal codes of ethics became necessary.
  • Compare the roles of ACM, IEEE-CS, and other bodies in shaping professional norms.
  • Identify recurring themes across historical codes (public interest, competence, honesty, fairness).
  • Relate historical failures to the content of modern codes.

Motivation

Codes of ethics are not decorative statements. They crystallize lessons from decades of accidents, scandals, and near-misses. Understanding how and why they were written helps practitioners apply them under real pressure instead of treating them as compliance checklists.

Historical Arc

Early computing was research and military work. As systems moved into banking, healthcare, aviation, and consumer life, failures began to affect the public directly. Professional societies responded with education standards, certification discussions, and formal codes that elevated public interest, competence, and honesty above pure technical cleverness.

Key organizations: ACM (Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct), IEEE Computer Society, and later joint efforts that influenced computing curricula worldwide.

Multi-Perspective View

Software Engineering

Codes reinforce process discipline, truthful status reporting, and refusing to release unsafe systems.

AI & Data Science

Modern interpretations extend classic principles to dataset bias, model opacity, and human oversight.

Cybersecurity

Responsible disclosure, authorization boundaries, and dual-use awareness appear as applications of honesty and public interest.

Business & Legal

Codes support duty-of-care arguments and can influence how organizations structure escalation and accountability.

Case Link — From Failures to Principles

Incidents such as Therac-25, the Ariane 5 flight 501 software exception, and later data-privacy scandals repeatedly showed that technical skill without professional norms produces preventable harm. Codes encode the corrective principles: prioritize public safety, maintain competence, communicate limitations honestly, and accept responsibility.

Summary

The computing profession developed codes of ethics in response to growing societal impact and repeated failures. Those codes remain living documents that guide judgment, training, and accountability across SE, AI, data, and security practice.

Cheat Sheet

  • Codes = shared professional expectations, not law
  • Public interest and competence are central themes
  • History of failure drives code content
  • Use codes as decision aids and communication tools

Glossary

Code of ethics
A formal statement of values and expected conduct for members of a profession.
Professionalization
The process by which an occupation develops standards, education, and self-regulation.
Public interest
The welfare of society as a whole, prioritized over narrow private gain in professional codes.
