Lecture 03 — Ethical Theories & Decision Frameworks

Classical ethical theories applied to computing decisions, plus practical frameworks for professional judgment.

Learning Objectives

  • Summarize utilitarianism, deontology, virtue ethics, and care ethics in computing contexts.
  • Apply at least two ethical theories to a realistic professional dilemma.
  • Use a structured decision framework (stakeholder analysis + principle check + consequence estimate).
  • Recognize limits of any single theory and the value of pluralistic reasoning.

Motivation

When a model disproportionately harms a protected group, or a release decision trades safety for schedule, intuition alone is unreliable. Ethical theories supply disciplined ways to examine consequences, duties, character, and relationships.

Core Theories

  • Utilitarianism: Choose the action that maximizes overall good / minimizes harm. Useful for impact assessment; risks ignoring minority rights.
  • Deontology: Focus on duties and rules (honesty, respect for persons, non-maleficence). Useful for non-negotiable obligations; can feel rigid under conflict.
  • Virtue ethics: Ask what a good professional would do; cultivate honesty, courage, humility, and care. Emphasizes character and habits.
  • Care / relational ethics: Attend to relationships, vulnerability, and context. Especially relevant in healthcare AI, education systems, and community platforms.

Multi-Perspective Application

Software Engineering

Duty to test adequately (deontology) vs. schedule pressure (utilitarian trade-offs); virtue of thoroughness.

AI & Data Science

Fairness metrics (utilitarian aggregation) vs. individual rights (deontology) vs. participatory design (care).

Cybersecurity

Responsible disclosure duties; limits on “ends justify the means” in offensive research.

Business & Legal

Codes and theories inform internal escalation and external regulatory expectations.

Scenario — Biased Hiring Model

A team discovers that a ranking model systematically downgrades candidates from certain demographic groups. Shipping on time would meet a major client deadline; delaying requires costly rework and explanation to leadership.

Apply each theory: What does maximizing aggregate utility suggest? What duties are engaged? What would a virtuous professional do? How does care for affected candidates change the analysis?

Reflection

  2. Which theory do you find most natural, and what blind spots might that create?

Summary

Ethical theories provide complementary lenses. Professionals combine them with stakeholder analysis and documented reasoning to make and defend decisions under uncertainty and pressure.

Cheat Sheet

  • Utilitarian → consequences / aggregate good
  • Deontological → duties and rights
  • Virtue → character and professional excellence
  • Care → relationships and vulnerability
  • Always: identify stakeholders and document reasoning
