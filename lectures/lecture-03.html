Lecture 03

Ethical Theories & Decision-Making Frameworks

Utilitarianism, deontology, virtue ethics, and practical frameworks for technology decisions.

⏱ ~55 min📚 Module 3 of 30
◎

Learning Objectives

  • Summarize consequentialist (utilitarian), duty-based (deontological), and virtue-based approaches.
  • Apply each lens to a concrete computing dilemma.
  • Recognize strengths and blind spots of each theory in socio-technical contexts.
  • Use a structured decision process that combines multiple perspectives.
⚖️

Three Classic Lenses

Utilitarianism

Focus on outcomes: maximize overall well-being (or minimize harm) for the greatest number. In computing: risk–benefit analysis, A/B testing impact, aggregate metrics.

Deontology

Focus on duties and rules: some actions are right or wrong regardless of consequences (e.g., respect for persons, honesty, keeping promises). In computing: informed consent, non-deception, respecting rights.

Virtue Ethics

Focus on character: what would a good professional do? Cultivates habits of honesty, courage, care, and justice. In computing: integrity under pressure, humility about uncertainty.

Practice tip
Real decisions rarely yield to a single theory. Use multiple lenses to surface different considerations, then deliberate.
🧩

Applied Scenario

An AI hiring tool improves average screening speed but shows lower recall for candidates from certain demographic groups. A utilitarian analysis might emphasize overall efficiency gains. A deontological analysis might emphasize fairness duties and non-discrimination. A virtue analysis might ask whether deploying the tool reflects care and justice. How would you structure the team’s discussion?
