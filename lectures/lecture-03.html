Module 03

Ethical Theories & Frameworks

Classic ethical theories as practical lenses for computing: consequences, duties, character, and care—applied to product, data, AI, and security decisions.

Reading ~40 minCases + Quiz
🎯

Learning Objectives

  • Explain utilitarianism, deontology, virtue ethics, and care ethics in plain language.
  • Apply each lens to the same computing scenario and compare conclusions.
  • Recognize limits of single-theory analysis and when to combine frameworks.
  • Use structured ethical analysis in design reviews and incident retrospectives.
📖

Introduction

Ethical theories do not replace professional codes; they help you reason when codes under-specify a case. Computing decisions often mix uncertain outcomes, conflicting duties, character pressures, and relationships of power. Multiple lenses reduce blind spots.

🧭

Four Working Lenses

Utilitarian

Compare expected benefits and harms across stakeholders. Ask who gains, who loses, and how severe and reversible harms are.

Deontological

Identify duties and rights that should not be traded away lightly—honesty, informed consent, privacy, non-discrimination.

Virtue

Ask what a careful, courageous, just professional would do—and what habits the organization is training.

Care ethics

Attend to vulnerability, dependence, and relationships. Who is least able to exit or protest the system?

🏭

One Scenario, Four Lenses

A recommendation system boosts engagement by amplifying polarizing content. Engagement metrics rise; moderation costs and user reports also rise. Apply each ethical lens and list at least one design change each lens would support.

✅

Knowledge Check

✍️

Reflection

Which ethical lens do you default to under time pressure? Which lens do you under-use? Give one example from study or work.

📋

Summary

  • Use multiple ethical theories to surface different risks and duties.
  • Consequence, duty, character, and care each catch failures the others miss.
  • Document analysis; pair theory with codes and organizational process.
