Lecture 03 · Privacy

Privacy, Data & Surveillance

Data lifecycle, consent, surveillance capitalism, PETs, and high-level regulatory ideas.

Learning Objectives

  • Map the data lifecycle and where privacy risk concentrates.
  • Explain consent limits and secondary use problems.
  • Distinguish privacy, confidentiality, and security.
  • Discuss professional duties when building surveillance-capable systems.

Motivation

Data is the fuel of modern computing. Collection is often cheap; harm from misuse, leakage, or secondary analysis can be irreversible. Professionals must treat personal data as a high-stakes asset, not a free byproduct of logging.

Core Distinctions

Privacy concerns control over personal information and the conditions of observation.

Confidentiality is an obligation to limit access to information entrusted to an organization.

Security is the set of technical and organizational measures that protect data against unauthorized access, alteration, or destruction.

Security is necessary but not sufficient for privacy. Perfectly secured data can still be used in ways that violate user expectations or dignity.

Real Story

Large-scale breaches (for example, Equifax) showed that weak security of sensitive personal data creates years of identity risk for millions of people. Parallel cases of lawful-but-controversial secondary use of data for political targeting illustrated that "we had access" is not the same as "we had moral or informed permission."

Professional Practice

  • Minimize collection and retention.
  • Purpose limitation: use data for the purpose disclosed to users.
  • Prefer privacy-enhancing techniques when feasible (aggregation, differential privacy concepts, access controls).
  • Document data flows so downstream teams cannot claim ignorance.

Knowledge Check

Reflection

Reflection

Identify one app you use daily. What data does it likely collect, and what secondary uses would you consider unacceptable even if legal?

Assignment

Draw a simple data-flow diagram for a fictional fitness app. Mark collection, storage, third-party sharing, and deletion. Write 200 words on the highest privacy risk and one mitigation.

Summary

Privacy is a professional design constraint. Treat personal data as high-risk material: minimize, purpose-limit, secure, and respect the human behind the record.
