Lecture 04 · IP

Intellectual Property & Open Source

Copyright, patents, trade secrets, licenses, FOSS culture, and professional obligations around IP.

Learning Objectives

  • Distinguish copyright, patents, trademarks, and trade secrets at a conceptual level.
  • Explain why software licenses matter for reuse and distribution.
  • Describe professional duties when using third-party and open-source code.
  • Recognize common license families (permissive vs. copyleft) without treating this as legal advice.

Motivation

Almost every modern product depends on other people's code, data, and designs. Ignoring IP rules creates legal risk, breaks community trust, and can destroy careers. Understanding licenses is part of professional competence.

Core Concepts

Copyright protects original expression (including source code) automatically in many jurisdictions.

Patents protect inventions that meet criteria of novelty and non-obviousness; software patent practice varies by region.

Trade secrets protect confidential business information that has economic value from secrecy.

Licenses grant permissions under conditions. Open-source licenses grant broad rights if conditions are met.

Professional rule of thumb: Never copy code into a product without knowing the license. Document provenance. Prefer approved license lists when your organization maintains them.

Open Source Culture

Open source is both a licensing model and a social practice of collaboration, review, and shared maintenance. Professional contribution includes respecting attribution, reporting vulnerabilities responsibly, and not treating community projects as free labor without reciprocity.

Industrial Scenario

A teammate pastes a Stack Overflow snippet and an unlicensed GitHub gist into production. The product is about to ship. What is your professional response?

Knowledge Check

Reflection

Reflection

Have you ever reused code without checking its license? What process would you put in place for your next team project?

Assignment

Compare one permissive license (e.g., MIT) and one copyleft license (e.g., GPL) in 250–400 words: permissions, conditions, and implications for a commercial SaaS product.

Summary

IP literacy is professional hygiene. Know what you use, under which terms, and how attribution and distribution conditions bind your product and your team.
