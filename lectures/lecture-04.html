Module 04

ACM & IEEE Codes of Ethics — Deep Dive

Principles · Public interest · Conflicts · Application to modern systems

Learning Objectives

  • State the primary obligations highlighted in the ACM and IEEE codes toward the public, clients, employers, and colleagues
  • Map code principles to concrete design, deployment, and disclosure decisions
  • Analyze conflicts between employer loyalty and public interest using the codes
  • Compare aspirational principles with enforceable professional expectations
  • Apply code language to scenarios involving AI, data, and security

1. Motivation

Codes of ethics translate general professional ideals into statements that members of ACM, IEEE, and related societies can use for education, self-assessment, and (in limited cases) peer accountability. They are not a substitute for law, but they shape what "reasonable professional practice" means in many contexts.

2. High-Level Structure of the Codes

Both ACM and IEEE codes emphasize:

  • Public interest and welfare — prioritize safety, security, and human well-being
  • Honesty and integrity — accurate claims about capabilities, limitations, and risks
  • Competence — work within areas of knowledge; maintain skills
  • Fairness and non-discrimination — avoid unjust bias in systems and workplaces
  • Privacy and confidentiality — respect personal data and sensitive information
  • Collegiality and professional development — support learning and constructive critique

Exact wording and organization differ by version and society; practitioners should consult the current official texts. This module focuses on shared professional expectations rather than clause-by-clause quotation.

3. Case Application — Algorithmic Hiring Tool

Scenario

A company deploys a résumé-screening model that improves efficiency but shows lower callback rates for candidates from certain demographic groups. The model was trained on historical hiring data. Product leadership wants to keep the system live while "monitoring." Engineering has evidence of disparate impact but incomplete causal understanding.

Code-oriented questions: What duties to the public and to fairness apply? What does competence require before claiming the system is ready? When does continued deployment without mitigation conflict with honesty about limitations?

4. Multi-Perspective View

Software Engineering

Codes reinforce requirements for adequate testing, clear communication of residual risk, and refusal to ship known dangerous defects when harm is foreseeable.

AI & Data Science

Principles on fairness, transparency, and accountability map directly onto documentation, evaluation metrics beyond accuracy, and human oversight for high-stakes decisions.

Cybersecurity

Confidentiality, authorized access, and responsible disclosure align with long-standing code themes; dual-use research adds tension between openness and harm prevention.

Leadership

Managers who are computing professionals still carry code obligations; organizational culture that punishes escalation conflicts with duties to the public.

Summary

  • ACM and IEEE codes operationalize duties to the public, clients, employers, and the profession.
  • Core themes: public welfare, honesty, competence, fairness, privacy, and collegiality.
  • Application requires interpretation in context—especially for AI, data, and security systems.
  • Codes support education and professional identity; they are not a complete substitute for law or organizational governance.
