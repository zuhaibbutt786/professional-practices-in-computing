Lecture 05 — Professional Responsibility & Accountability

Individual and collective responsibility, accountability mechanisms, escalation, and learning from failure.

Learning Objectives

  • Distinguish responsibility, accountability, and liability in professional contexts.
  • Identify appropriate escalation paths when ethical or safety concerns arise.
  • Analyze how organizational culture enables or blocks responsible practice.
  • Apply lessons from well-known computing failures to personal and team practice.

Responsibility vs Accountability

Responsibility is the obligation to act with care and to own the consequences of one’s role. Accountability is the obligation to answer for those actions to others (teams, managers, regulators, the public). Professionals often share responsibility across roles; clear accountability prevents diffusion.

Escalation and Speaking Up

When residual risk, bias, or pressure to misrepresent appears, professionals need channels: peer review, technical leads, ethics officers, anonymous reporting, and, in extreme cases, external disclosure consistent with law and professional codes. Retaliation-free cultures are essential.

Multi-Perspective View

Software Engineering

Code ownership, review culture, and post-incident learning support collective responsibility.

AI & Data Science

Model cards, impact assessments, and monitoring make residual risk visible and reviewable.

Cybersecurity

Responsible disclosure and incident response require clear roles and communication.

Leadership

Leaders set incentives and psychological safety that determine whether concerns surface early.

Scenario — Near-Miss Safety Report

An engineer discovers a race condition that could, under rare conditions, produce incorrect outputs in a medical decision-support tool. The release is scheduled for the next day. Document the immediate actions, stakeholders to inform, and how accountability should be assigned for the decision to ship or delay.
