Lecture 05

Professional Responsibility, Accountability & Whistleblowing

Duty of care, liability, escalation paths, and the ethics of speaking up.

⏱ ~55 min📚 Module 5 of 30
◎

Learning Objectives

  • Distinguish responsibility, accountability, and liability in professional contexts.
  • Describe duty of care in software and data systems.
  • Outline internal and external escalation options when serious risks are ignored.
  • Discuss ethical and practical dimensions of whistleblowing.
🛡️

Responsibility vs Accountability

Responsibility is the obligation to act (or refrain) according to role and professional standards. Accountability is the obligation to answer for outcomes — to explain, justify, and accept consequences. In complex systems, many people share responsibility; clear accountability structures are essential so that “everyone’s job” does not become “no one’s job.”

Diffusion of responsibility
Large organizations and multi-vendor stacks can obscure who is answerable. Professionals should seek clarity on decision rights and escalation paths early.
📢

Speaking Up & Whistleblowing

When internal channels fail to address serious risk (safety, privacy, fraud, discrimination), professionals may face a choice about external disclosure. Factors include:

  • Severity and likelihood of harm
  • Whether internal escalation was attempted in good faith
  • Legal protections and risks in the relevant jurisdiction
  • Personal and career consequences

Codes of ethics generally support raising concerns; they do not prescribe a single path. Document carefully, seek confidential advice when possible, and understand local law.

🏭

Industrial Scenario

You discover that a production model used in loan decisions has a documented fairness issue that was deprioritized for a release deadline. Your manager says the risk is “acceptable.” What steps would you take before considering external channels?
