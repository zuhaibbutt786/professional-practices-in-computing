About the Course

What you will learn

Professional ethics, responsibility, computing law, privacy, AI ethics, open source, intellectual property, communication, teamwork, leadership, sustainability, and career readiness — independent of any specific programming language or tool.

How you will learn

Case-based and scenario-based learning, real industrial incidents, reflection, interactive knowledge checks, decision simulators, and progressive assignments designed for active and flipped-classroom pedagogy.

Who it is for

Undergraduate students in Computer Science, Software Engineering, Data Science, Artificial Intelligence, Cybersecurity, and Information Technology who need ACM/IEEE-aligned professional competencies.

Learning Outcomes

  • Apply major ethical frameworks to contemporary computing dilemmas (privacy, AI, security, IP).
  • Interpret and use professional codes (ACM, IEEE) in concrete workplace scenarios.
  • Analyze legal and regulatory dimensions of software, data, and AI systems at a high level.
  • Communicate technical and ethical reasoning clearly in writing and presentations.
  • Practice leadership, conflict resolution, and remote collaboration skills.
  • Design with accessibility, inclusion, safety, and sustainability in mind.
  • Plan continuous professional development and personal branding for a computing career.

Weekly Modules

01

Introduction & Professional Identity

What it means to be a computing professional; roles, responsibilities, and the social contract of the profession.

 02

Ethics Frameworks & Decision Making

Utilitarianism, deontology, virtue ethics, and structured ethical decision processes applied to technology cases.

 03

Privacy, Data & Surveillance

Data lifecycle, consent, surveillance capitalism, privacy-enhancing technologies, and regulatory landscapes.

 04

Intellectual Property & Open Source

Copyright, patents, trade secrets, licenses, FOSS culture, and professional obligations around IP.

 05

Professional Codes & Responsibility

ACM and IEEE codes in depth; whistleblowing, accountability, and organizational ethics.

Professional Codes

ACM Code of Ethics

General ethical principles, professional responsibilities, and leadership principles for computing professionals.

IEEE Code of Ethics

Commitment to safety, honesty, fairness, and continuous improvement in engineering and technology practice.

Career & Lifelong Learning

Personal branding, interviews, certifications, networking, freelancing ethics, and continuous skill development are woven throughout the modules and will expand in later lectures.

FAQ

No. It teaches professional thinking and judgment independent of any programming language or software stack.

Can I use this for university teaching?

Yes. Content is original synthesis intended for educational use 2026–2035. Always consult primary sources for formal citation.

How is progress tracked?
